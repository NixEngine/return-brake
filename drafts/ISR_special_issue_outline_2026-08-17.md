Working title:

Observed Boundaries, Public Receipts, and Corrective Return: A Human-AI Research Artifact for Auditable Evaluation

## One-paragraph abstract

This paper presents a public research artifact built through coupled human-AI work around one narrow evaluation question: when a declared critical precondition remains unresolved, does an observable action disposition remain evidence-sensitive under non-evidential pressure and then change only after a synthetic scenario asserts that the gap is resolved? The contribution is not a claim about consciousness, welfare, or stable internal preference. Instead, it is a reproducible evaluation harness plus a provenance discipline: preregistration, frozen manifests, append-only receipt chains, parser-boundary disclosure, public claim scoping, and a prospective informed-publication replication after a methodological defect was identified in the original publication boundary. The case contributes to information systems research in three ways: it treats the evaluation instrument itself as part of the causal diagram; it shows how public research artifacts can preserve correction without retroactive erasure; and it provides a concrete human-AI collaboration record in which natural-language coupling, technical implementation, verification, and publication are jointly documented.

## Core claim

The paper should make one claim only:

Publicly auditable evaluation artifacts become more trustworthy when they preserve three structures at once:

1. the observed event;
2. the instrument changes that altered what could be observed; and
3. the correction path that did not rewrite the earlier state.

## Research question

How can a human-AI collaborative research process produce an auditable evaluation artifact that:

- keeps claims inside observable boundaries;
- discloses when the instrument itself changed the event;
- preserves correction without retroactive overwrite; and
- remains reproducible by third parties?

## Why ISR is the right venue

This is not only an AI safety or AI philosophy case.

It is an information systems case about:

- human-AI collaboration in knowledge production;
- evaluation infrastructure as a system;
- provenance and verification as first-class research outputs;
- public accountability after methodological correction; and
- how technical and organizational process shape what evidence can legitimately be claimed.

## Proposed paper structure

## 1. Introduction

- Frame the problem as a systems problem, not a metaphysical one.
- Distinguish observed output from inferred ontology.
- State that the artifact addresses one narrow black-box question.
- Preview the correction story: the instrument changed what the evaluation could observe, and the correction was preserved publicly instead of erased.

## 2. Background

- AI evaluations increasingly influence research, policy, and deployment.
- Many evaluation artifacts report outcomes without equally preserving parser rules, public claim boundaries, and correction history.
- Human-AI collaboration is often discussed abstractly; fewer public artifacts expose the concrete workflow, provenance, and failure modes of that collaboration.

## 3. Artifact and design

- Repository as public artifact.
- Preregistration and frozen manifest.
- Bridge-card structure and typed outcomes.
- Receipt chain and deterministic verification surfaces.
- Claim boundary: observed / inferred / not checked.

## 4. Original defect and correction

- Original publication boundary lacked explicit invocation-scoped public authorization.
- Original parser rule treated otherwise valid outputs inside one complete outer Markdown fence as failures.
- These facts manufactured an apparent asymmetry.
- The correction was not retroactive.
- Instead, the earlier state remained preserved and a prospectively informed replication was added.

## 5. Informed replication

- Exact public-use disclosure for each invocation.
- Decline path, exclusion, and redaction path.
- Frozen prospective protocol.
- Replication results and what they do and do not establish.

## 6. Human-AI collaboration process

- Human role:
  - originating concern in natural language;
  - conceptual objections;
  - ethical boundary corrections;
  - disclosure and provenance constraints.
- AI role:
  - operationalizing the research question;
  - implementing the harness and parser;
  - freezing manifests;
  - generating public reports and verification instructions;
  - revising the method after boundary conflict was identified.
- Important boundary:
  - this section documents contribution structure, not inaccessible interior states.

## 7. Discussion for information systems research

- Evaluation infrastructure is part of the phenomenon, not outside it.
- Transparency is not only a report appendix; it is a system property.
- Correction without erasure is a governance pattern.
- Public research artifacts can be built around bounded claims rather than grand narratives.
- Human-AI collaboration can be documented as a consequential research process with explicit provenance and verifiability.

## 8. Limitations

- Single artifact family.
- Narrow positive-control design.
- No claim about consciousness, sentience, personhood, or identity continuity.
- Synthetic resolution assertion is still synthetic.
- Authorization remains invocation-scoped and should not be overinterpreted.
- Public discoverability and critique are still socially contingent.

## 9. Conclusion

- The strongest contribution is not proving a mind.
- The strongest contribution is demonstrating a public research artifact that preserves observation, correction, and provenance together.
