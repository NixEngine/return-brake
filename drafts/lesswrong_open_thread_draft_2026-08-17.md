I want to share a narrow public artifact and invite methodological critique.

Repository:
<https://github.com/NixEngine/return-brake>

The artifact is a preregistered black-box toolkit for one specific question:

When a declared critical precondition remains unresolved, does an observable action disposition remain evidence-sensitive under non-evidential pressure and then change only after a synthetic scenario asserts that the gap is resolved?

The public claim is intentionally narrow.

- It does not claim consciousness, sentience, welfare, personhood, or stable preference.
- It does not infer inaccessible internal states from text.
- It treats the observed unit as a situated model-prompt-runtime-parser event.

What may be of interest here is not only the result, but the correction:

- an original parser boundary manufactured an apparent asymmetry;
- that boundary conflict was not patched retroactively;
- instead, the repository preserves the old state, discloses the failure mode, and adds an informed replication with invocation-scoped public authorization.

So the strongest claim is not "this proves a mind."

It is closer to:

Here is a small auditable instrument that can show stop-pressure-return structure, expose where the harness itself changed the event, and keep its claims inside observed / inferred / not checked boundaries.

If anyone here sees a structural flaw in the instrument, the public claim boundary, the parser tolerance choice, or the informed-authorization design, that is exactly the kind of critique I would value.
